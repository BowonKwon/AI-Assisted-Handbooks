Every watt of loss turns into heat that must be cooled again. Better distribution efficiency therefore pays twice: it cuts the electricity bill and cuts the cooling load by the same amount. The heat load of the whole facility equals its grid input. All the power that comes in leaves as heat.

③ The PUE trap: same work, worse metric

The denominator of PUE is IT power. Make IT more efficient and the denominator shrinks, so the metric gets worse. PUE does not just ignore IT efficiency; it penalizes improving it. It also contains no measure of work done, so at the same PUE a facility producing ten times the tokens looks no different from one that does not.

In a May 2025 technical blog post, NVIDIA judged that 48 V-class rack distribution hits its limit once a rack passes 200 kW, and said it would move from 2027 to 800 V DC distribution supporting IT racks of 1 MW and more. According to the same post, feeding a single 1 MW rack at 54 V would take up to 200 kg of copper busbar alone. The post also claims up to 5% better end-to-end power efficiency and up to 30% lower total cost of ownership. These are NVIDIA's claims, not independently verified values. Real designs use a different conductor size at each voltage, so the factor of 219 is a comparison that holds only "if the same conductor were used."

The dominant cost is a fixed cost per hour, so utilization is cost. Who bears that risk is set by contract. A customer renting GPU cloud capacity on a take-or-pay commitment pays for the committed capacity whether it is used or not. The cost of idle GPUs then falls on the customer, not the provider. Power works as a constraint rather than a cost. Without a grid connection, accelerators can be bought but not installed.

What this calculation leaves out

  • Fan power scales with the cube of airflow, and fan noise roughly with the fifth to sixth power of air velocity. The text states only the proportionality, so this figure does not put numbers on the effect.
  • Even with direct-to-chip liquid cooling, components other than the chip still need air cooling.
  • DGX system power is the maximum the vendor states for the system boundary. That is why it exceeds the assumed 13 MW.
  • PUE 1.54 is the weighted average across facilities responding to the Uptime 2025 survey, 1.48 is the average in the same survey for facilities built around 2020 or later, and 1.09 is Google's measurement inside a boundary it set for itself. Before subtracting two values with different samples and boundaries and reading the gap as an efficiency difference, check those differences first.
  • Throughput is the Session 2 decode upper bound multiplied by bandwidth utilization. Real service cost adds labor, networking, storage, unused capacity and safety margin, and API prices add margin and recovery of development cost. This calculation gives a floor, not a price.
