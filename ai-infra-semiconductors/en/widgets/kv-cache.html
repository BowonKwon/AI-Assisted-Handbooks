The weights and the KV cache share one accelerator's memory. The capacity left over sets the batch size, and the batch size sets the arithmetic intensity. Change the sequence length and the precision and watch how a capacity limit ends up holding down bandwidth efficiency as well.

What this calculation leaves out

  • Utilization is counted as Bsz / I*(BF16). If computation also runs at the weight precision, the actual arithmetic intensity 2Bsz/b and that precision's ridge point (2/b)·I* grow together, so their ratio equals this value (table in Session 2, solution to Exercise 3). If only the weights are read at low precision and computation runs in BF16, arithmetic intensity rises by a factor of 2/b and utilization comes out that much higher (this is the case the precision buttons on the roofline bench show).
  • The throughput ceiling counts only the bandwidth side. Once the batch size passes the ridge point the workload becomes compute-bound, so this formula overstates the ceiling. The page flags that case separately.
  • Traffic for reading the KV cache is left out. With long contexts the KV cache must also be read at every step, so real speed is lower.
  • Every sequence is assumed to fill up to the maximum length S. With schemes like PagedAttention that allocate only what is used, the average length is what counts.
  • Capacity is counted in decimal GB (109 bytes), as in the text.
