How many years is 1,000 hours?

Failures accelerated by temperature are usually converted from test time to service time with the Arrhenius equation. Under the same conditions, changing Ea alone swings the conclusion a lot.

AF = exp[ (Ea / k) · (1/Tuse − 1/Tstress) ]　·　k = 8.617×10−5 eV/K　·　T = °C + 273.15　·　equivalent years = test hours × AF / 8,760

What this calculation leaves out

  • Ea differs from one failure mechanism to another. That is why a single test cannot tell you "this chip will last 9 years."
  • JESD47I also cautions that this period may be shorter than actual use requirements and depends on the failure mechanism and the use environment, and it says long-term wear-out mechanisms should be covered by wafer-level reliability data.
  • The equation applies to temperature-accelerated failures. JEDEC JEP122 collects the acceleration models for each failure mechanism.
  • JESD47I, in a note to Table 1, uses the same conditions (0.7 eV, 125 °C → 55 °C) as an example and gives an acceleration factor of 78.6, about 9 years. The last digits differ because of constants and rounding.
