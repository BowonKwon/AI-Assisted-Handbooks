Slip one in front and one behind — the sandwich seen from the victim's side

Three trades go, in order, into Chapter 15's constant-product pool: ① the attacker buys X with Y, ② the victim buys X at the already raised price, ③ the attacker sells the X back. This figure shows only what the victim receives less of, and by how much. The units are numbers of tokens in the pool, not currency.

What is not in this figure

  • How much the attacker takes. Chapter 17's NB17-1 keeps that column to show the structure, but this figure draws only the victim's side. What the attacker takes is in the end the Y the victim paid, and putting in capital larger than the victim's order leaves nothing more to take (Chapter 17, Section 5).
  • The auction that buys and sells gas, priority fees and the order within a block. Who sets the order is in Chapter 17, Section 3; proposer–builder separation is in Section 4.
  • Narrowing the tolerance shrinks the attack that can be slipped in, but it also raises the chance that one's own trade fails. That trade-off is in the victim's hands (NB17-2).
  • These are not the values of any real market. Only structural variables in pool units move.
