The raw bytes of the genesis block header (the bundled snapshot) are colored as six fields. Select a cell to see its value with the little-endian order unpacked. SHA-256 is applied twice to the whole header and the result is matched to the published block hash. You can also see what happens when the nonce changes.

What this anatomy viewer does not do

  • It does not query the network. The bytes are a snapshot obtained from two block explorers on the reference date (2026-10-25) and bundled with the book (the two responses are byte-for-byte identical), and the published hash is the assert value in the Bitcoin Core source.
  • It handles only the header. The transaction (the coinbase and the sentence inside it; txid = Merkle root) is unpacked from the whole 285 bytes by NB12-3 in Chapter 12.
  • Changing the nonce and mining again is the job of the hand miner in Chapter 8 (W2). Here we only go as far as seeing that changing the nonce by one breaks the condition — finding the nonce 2083236893 on January 3, 2009 took billions of hashes on average, and verifying it takes two.
