Chapter 24 · Stablecoins

The collateral ratio is a number about solvency, the redeemable ratio a number about liquidity

Set the composition of reserve assets per 1 of issuance and choose how heavily redemption demands bunch up. If the value that can be disposed of right now does not cover the demand, the price slides, and with the price below 1, more holders come forward to redeem — this feedback is a simple rule of this book's own. The types are illustrations of structure, not the books of any coin.

CR = A/S · RR(q) = Σ ℓiAi(1−hi) / (qS) · rule: if RR ≥ 1, redemption at par; otherwise price → RR, q ← q + 1.5·(1−price)·(1−q) · window closed: price −0.05/round

What this model is not

  • It is not the books of any coin. The weights, ℓ and h are illustrations of structure, and the feedback by which a falling price brings on redemptions (sensitivity 1.5) is not a reported fact but a modeling assumption of this book (Chapter 24, Section 5).
  • Closing the window fails to stop the run as a consequence of assuming a secondary market. The basis for that assumption is the Federal Reserve note (December 2025) quoted in Chapter 24; the numbers are not from it.
  • The reflexive feedback of the algorithmic type (a promise to exchange into a second token) is a different model — Chapter 24, NB24-3.
  • A round is not a unit of time. The six rounds are only a count that shows "how many times the feedback goes around."
