Chapter 15 · Decentralized exchanges and AMMs

The larger the pool, the smaller the slippage on the same order

Put Δx of X into a constant-product pool with reserves x and y: how much Y do you receive? The only variables are the pool size and the order size, and the units are numbers of tokens in the pool. What the tokens are, and what they are worth in any currency, are not in this figure.

Δy = y·(1−γ)·Δx / (x + (1−γ)·Δx), γ = 0.003 · marginal price y/x · slippage = 1 − (Δy/Δx)/(y/x) · the fee stays in the pool and k = xy grows

Impermanent loss — the pool's value ratio by price multiple r (assuming no fee and full correction by arbitrage)
Vpool/Vhold = 2√r/(1+r)
Impermanent loss = 2√r/(1+r) − 1

It is a loss whichever the direction, and larger the larger the move. Whether the fees make up for it is a separate number that depends on trading volume (Chapter 15, NB15-2), and this book does not call supplying liquidity "profit."

What this figure does not say

  • What the tokens are, and what one of them is worth in any currency. Slippage is set only by "the ratio of the order size to the pool size" — which is why there is no price on the axes.
  • Whether fee income or impermanent loss is the larger. That is an empirical question that depends on trading volume and price movement (NB15-2); this figure shows only the shape of the formulas.
  • A spot price is not an oracle. The ratio in the pool moves with a single large order — Chapters 16 and 17.
  • Concentrated liquidity (v3) behaves, within the same range, like a pool with larger reserves. That curve is in the [Advanced] box of Chapter 15 and is not drawn here.
